- `data/industry_1940.json`, `data/docks_1940.json` and `data/landmarks_1940.json` (the 1940s industry, waterfront and landmark datasets) are **hand-authored** — generated by `tools/make_industry_1940.py` from general world knowledge of each site's real location. No third-party dataset, gazetteer or OSM extract was used, so they carry no licence encumbrance.
- `data/sea_defences_1940.json` and `data/dams_1940.json` (the 1940s sea-defence and Dam Busters datasets) are **hand-authored** — generated by `tools/make_sea_defences_1940.py` and `tools/make_dams_1940.py`. Every row records the cited source for its real WGS84 position in the file's `source` field. The six dams' coordinates were verified against Wikipedia articles (CC BY-SA 4.0, https://www.wikipedia.org): *Möhne Reservoir*, *Edersee Dam*, *Sorpe Dam*, *Ennepetalsperre*, *Listertalsperre*, *Diemelsee*. Sea-fort positions cite Wikipedia *Maunsell Forts* (Rough Sands / Sunk Head / Nore), *Grain Tower* and *Nore*; the Zeebrugge/Ostend blockships cite *Zeebrugge Raid*; the Gooseberry breakwater hulks cite *Mulberry harbour*; the mine-barrage, harbour-boom and beach-obstacle lines follow documented 1939–45 defensive history. No third-party geometry, imagery or data file was copied for these two files.
- `data/decoys_1940.json` (the 1940s bombing-decoy dataset) is **hand-authored** — generated by `tools/make_decoys_1940.py`. Every row records the cited source for its real WGS84 position in the file's `source` field. Sources: the Airfield Research Group airfield bombing-decoy listing (https://www.airfieldresearchgroup.org.uk); Suffolk Heritage Explorer (https://heritage.suffolk.gov.uk) records RMS 042 (Shottisham, Civil Starfish + QL) and HLY 098 (Hollesley Common, K+Q decoy airfield); Historic England Research Records (https://www.heritagegateway.org.uk) record Airfield Bombing Decoy Q42a (Nazeing, RAF North Weald); Heritage Gateway / NHER 13606 (Winterton Ness, SF/QL decoy for the Great Yarmouth naval base). The decoy-programme background (K/Q/QF/SF kinds) follows Wikipedia *Starfish site* (CC BY-SA 4.0). Where a source gives only an OSGB National Grid reference, it was converted to WGS84 with the standard OS "A guide to coordinate systems in Great Britain" OSGB36→WGS84 Helmert transform; the grid reference is quoted verbatim in the record's `source`.
- `data/pylons_1940.json` (the curated 1927–35 National Grid period-grid fallback) is **hand-authored** — generated by `tools/make_pylons_1940.py`. Each line links two real, cited CEB / National Grid places and records its source per row. Sources: Wikipedia *National Grid (GB)* / *Central Electricity Board* (CC BY-SA 4.0); Grace's Guide *Central Electricity Board* (https://www.gracesguide.co.uk) for Northfleet, the largest 132 kV switching station in the South-East England scheme. The **primary** power layer is the real OpenStreetMap `power=line` data fetched to `data/osm/power.json` (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL, https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright); the curated file is the fallback used only when that fetch is unavailable.
- `data/rail_drama_1940.json` (the curated railway-drama viaduct/tunnel catalogue) is **hand-authored** — generated by `tools/make_rail_drama_1940.py`. - **Ocean3D Lite** (Gerstner wave field used by `shaders/water.gdshader`; the addon is vendored unmodified at `addons/ocean3d_lite/`), author **Justin Schroeder**, licence **MIT** (full text at `addons/ocean3d_lite/LICENSE.md`), source https://store.godotengine.org/asset/ocean3d/ocean3d-lite/ (Godot Asset Store). The sea's swell is a Gerstner field adapted from its `shaders/ocean.gdshader`: four crossing trains, each riding at its own deep-water phase speed `c = sqrt(g/k)`, evaluated on world XZ. Everything else in the sea (depth-driven colour from the terrain depth texture, Fresnel sky reflection, shoreline foam, alpha) is this project's own work, as is the decision to fade the displacement out beyond ~900 m because a theatre-sized water plane has no vertex density to carry swell to the horizon.
